This week, we’re taking a closer look at **Impact Hub** and the entrepreneurs working within its community. Its Euston home connects with a wider story about the *Knowledge Quarter*, where universities, research institutes, cultural organisations and businesses sit within walking distance of one another.

<!--read-->

#### From a warehouse to a working community

Before it became a workspace for social entrepreneurs, 34B York Way had housed a warehouse, manufacturing and car repairs. In October 2008, the building opened as Impact Hub King’s Cross, following the first Impact Hub in Islington in 2005.

The idea was to bring people working on social and environmental problems into a shared space, with access to advice, collaborators and business support. Over time, programmes developed around food, local entrepreneurship and the circular economy.

The organisation became Impact Hub London in 2023. A year later, it opened its [Euston workspace](https://london.impacthub.net/euston) at 1 Triton Square, through a partnership with British Land and the Knowledge Quarter. Members can use coworking space, offices and meeting rooms, alongside workshops, mentoring and networking opportunities.

#### Inside the Knowledge Quarter

The [Knowledge Quarter](https://www.knowledgequarter.london/) brings together organisations around King’s Cross, Euston Road and Bloomsbury. Established as a partnership in 2014, it connects institutions working across research, education, technology and culture, including the British Library, UCL, Wellcome and the Francis Crick Institute.

A knowledge cluster develops when organisations with different expertise gather in the same area. Researchers, entrepreneurs and cultural institutions may share skills, facilities and contacts. Being close enough to meet regularly can make those exchanges easier, although relationships still need time and effort.

Impact Hub London has a place within that network. Its Euston partnership aims to connect businesses, universities and local communities, giving smaller organisations opportunities to join conversations with larger institutions.

In April 2026, the Knowledge Quarter and London Society’s [Future London series](https://www.knowledgequarter.london/event/future-london-technology-health/) included a discussion at the hub about technology and healthcare. The programme brought together perspectives from the health and technology sectors alongside Londoners interested in the city’s future.

#### Turning purpose into a viable business

For a social enterprise, the commercial questions can be familiar: who will pay for the service, how much will it cost to deliver and how will the organisation keep going? Alongside these sit questions about the social or environmental changes it wants to achieve.

Impact Hub London’s [business programmes](https://london.impacthub.net/programmes) support entrepreneurs with mentoring, opportunities to test ideas and preparation for funding. Its work covers climate action, health and inclusion.

Past programmes have supported circular economy businesses and helped Camden and Islington residents develop enterprises addressing local health needs. Its current Together for Wellbeing programme supports entrepreneurs developing preventative mental health solutions.

Access is also part of the hub’s work. When it opened in Euston, it introduced free memberships for Camden residents and businesses, creating a route into the community for people who might otherwise struggle to afford workspace.

#### What our students explored

When we brought students to Impact Hub London Euston, Jo from Good Karma Media shared his experience of building a social impact communications agency.

The discussion covered the relationship between purpose and commercial viability, and how a business can grow its impact while sustaining its operations. Jo also explored how organisations communicate their work through storytelling.

For students exploring enterprise, these questions also reach into the wider neighbourhood. How does an idea find its customers? Who helps it develop? And how can a district rich in knowledge create opportunities for the people living around it?
