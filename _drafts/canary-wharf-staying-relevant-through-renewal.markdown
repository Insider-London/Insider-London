This week, we’re returning to Canary Wharf to explore the latest developments shaping one of London’s main business districts. JPMorganChase is planning a new headquarters, Citi has reopened its refurbished tower, and PwC is preparing for a move to One Eden.

<!--read-->

#### JPMorganChase: plans for a new headquarters

In November 2025, [JPMorganChase announced plans](https://via.tt.se/pressmeddelande/4162206/jpmorganchase-announces-intention-to-build-a-new-three-million-sq-ft-landmark-tower-in-london?lang=en) for a headquarters with three million square feet of space at Riverside South. Designed by Foster + Partners, it would accommodate up to 12,000 employees. The bank expects construction to take six years once the necessary approvals and agreements are secured. Its plans also depend on a favourable business environment in the UK.

The tower is still at the planning stage: the [project’s indicative timetable](https://riverside-south.com/) points to an application in the first half of 2027; however, the scale suggests confidence in London’s role in global finance. For businesses deciding where to invest, the proposal offers a view of the district’s ambitions over the coming decade. The surrounding streets and waterfront are part of those plans.  These would help link Riverside South to the rest of the estate. For the local residents, better routes and places to sit could make the development useful even if they never enter the bank’s offices.

The financial impact would extend beyond banking. A study commissioned by JPMorgan estimates that the development, together with improvements to its existing offices, could contribute £9.9 billion to the UK economy over six years. The same study forecasts more than 7,800 jobs across construction and other local industries.


#### Citi: investing in an existing tower

At Citi, renewal is already becoming part of the working day. On 21 September 2026, [the bank opened its refurbished London headquarters](https://www.citigroup.com/global/news/press-release/2026/citi-opens-redeveloped-london-headquarters-marking-major-milestone-in-firms-long-term-investment-uk), with the first employees returning to the tower. Further teams are moving in during the remainder of the year, with around 10,000 colleagues eventually expected to work there.

Citi's **$1.5 billion** project is firm evidence of its lasting commitment to the UK. The investment gives that statement substance: the bank has spent years adapting an existing building to support its operations.

The refurbishment began in 2023. Citi says the new layout and technology will help teams work together and serve clients. Its investment also supported UK suppliers and specialist manufacturers. For Canary Wharf, keeping a major employer involves making familiar premises work for changing needs. A building retains its place in the skyline while becoming quite different inside.

#### PwC: a new future for One Eden

PwC’s plans extend the story beyond banking to accountancy and consulting services. In July 2026, [the firm announced a preliminary agreement, known as heads of terms,](https://www.pwc.co.uk/press-room/press-releases/corporate-news/one-eden-canary-wharf.html) for 350,000 square feet at One Eden, formerly 33 Canada Square. The move is planned for 2030, ahead of refurbishment at its Embankment Place offices. PwC will also continue to occupy More London.

One Eden connects that commercial decision with the environmental case for renewal. [Canary Wharf Group’s refurbishment plans](https://cwg.com/press-release/cwg-launches-one-eden-300326/) would retain 97% of the existing foundations and building structure, preserving more than 62,000 tonnes of concrete. The building is designed to run entirely on electricity, using heat pumps. PwC has highlighted *flexibility, facilities and sustainability* among the reasons for choosing One Eden.
